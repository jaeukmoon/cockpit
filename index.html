Strategy & cost assumptions

Monthly selection of 10 Korean stocks, with optimized allocations, weekly market-risk and position-exit checks, and rule-based partial profit taking. Long-only, unlevered, with a 20% allocation cap at rebalance; weights may drift afterward.

The base case applies a 0.0140527% brokerage fee on each buy and sell, plus 0.20% sell tax, with no additional slippage. These fixed fee and tax assumptions apply throughout the historical simulation; they do not reconstruct historical tax changes or personal account charges. Stock-exit signals use a one-session information lag.

Investment process

Rules before opinions.

The public case study explains the reproducible process without exposing account balances, current holdings, or proprietary factor weights.

01 · UNIVERSE

Start with Korean small caps

Define the investable universe using market size, liquidity, and data-availability rules before any company is ranked.

02 · SCORE

Combine six factor signals

Rank companies across value, profitability, and growth dimensions. The data and knowledge date are part of the strategy contract.

03 · CONSTRUCT

Rebalance monthly

Build a diversified portfolio from the highest-ranked candidates and apply the same selection schedule consistently.

04 · CONTROL

Track risk explicitly

Record every monthly result, monitor exits with deterministic rules, and keep research, paper, shadow, and live claims separate.

What the number means

Transparent by design.

  • +21.82% is the geometric compounding of ten monthly live-account returns from July 2025 through April 2026.
  • Brokerage fees are reflected. No profit amount, starting capital, account balance, or current holding is published.
  • The result is not annualized. Backtest performance is not mixed into the live track record.
  • No market-outperformance claim is made until a comparable benchmark series and return convention are verified.
  • This is a personal research project, not investment advice. Past performance does not guarantee future results.
